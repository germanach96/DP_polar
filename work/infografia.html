01Forecast rule

Fragrances.

Keep last year's shape. Apply this year's direction.
BASE= same month last year + 25% of its supply cuts − 50% of its positive DAs
FORECAST= BASE × (1 + 12-month trend of house × size)
e.g. 1,000 shipped last October  +50 cuts  −50 DAs  → base 1,000  × 0.90 (trend −10%)  = 900
  • Trend = last 12 closed months ÷ the 12 before − 1, on actual shipments only. Capped at ±30%.
  • Size groups: minis ≤15 ml · 20–40 · 45–60 · 75–125 · 150 ml+ / refills · ancillaries.
  • In the trend: every Central item, launches included once they have 6 months of shipments.
  • Every month: recalculate the rule with the last 12 closed months and give the next 9 months.

Why each piece is there
01
Last year's month as the base
Fragrance repeats the same calendar every year: retailers load for Christmas from July to September. Last year's month already carries that shape.
02
A 12-month trend
A 3-month window jumps with every supply cut or shifted order. Twelve months cover a full season, so what moves is the real direction.
03
Grouped by house × size
One EAN's trend is noise: a big order or a delisting flips it. Pooling products of the same house and size averages that noise out, and size adds a bit of real difference.
04
Launches inside the trend
New products partly replace old ones. Leaving them out shows only the decline of the old range.
05
Only 25% of the cuts
A cut month understates demand, so some of it goes back. But retailers re-order weekly while an item is cut, so most of the figure is duplicated.
06
Remove 50% of the positive DAs
Last year's promotions should not repeat by default. Only half, because part of the DAs are market inputs that do come back.
07
Recalculate every month
Each month the 12-month window moves forward one month, so the trend and the base already include the latest reality. No manual corrections needed.
Local items (under 6 months of shipments) keep the consensus. Items with 6–17 months of life lean more on consensus: their last year includes the launch pipeline fill.
02Forecast rule

Makeup.

No calendar to copy. Follow the current pace.
MONTH= shipments + min(10% of cuts ; 10% of shipments) − 25% of positive DAs
BASE= average of the last 6 adjusted months
FORECAST= BASE × (1 + ½ × 12-month trend of Face · Lips · Eyes) same every month
e.g. 1,000 shipped  +100 cuts (capped)  −50 DAs  → 1,050 a month  × 0.90 (½ of −20%)  = 945 every month
  • Category = Face, Lips or Eyes within each brand family.
  • Trend = last 12 closed months ÷ the 12 before − 1, actual shipments only. Apply half of it, capped at ±30%.
  • In the average: every Central item (6+ months of shipments).
  • Every month: recalculate the 6-month average and the trend and give the next 9 months.

Why each piece is there
01
No seasonality
Shoppers do buy more in December, but our shipments follow launches, pipeline fills and order timing, not the calendar. Copying last year's month copies noise.
02
Average of the last 6 months
It reflects the current selling pace and smooths one-off spikes, without chasing a single big order.
03
Same number for every month
With no stable monthly shape, a flat run-rate is the honest call. Peaks and dips get corrected as months arrive.
04
Only half of the trend
The 6-month average already holds part of the recent trend. The full trend on top would count it twice.
05
Only 10% of the cuts
When an item is cut, retailers keep re-ordering the same quantity week after week. In makeup that re-ordering is far heavier, so the cuts are heavily inflated.
06
Only 25% of the DAs
A six-month average already dilutes a single promotion. Removing more would cut growing lines too hard.
07
Recalculate, don't correct
A rolling average refreshes itself with the latest months every cycle. An extra correction factor would add noise.
Local items (under 6 months of shipments) keep the consensus.
